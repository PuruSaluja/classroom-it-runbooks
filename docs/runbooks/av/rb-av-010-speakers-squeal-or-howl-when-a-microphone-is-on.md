# Speakers squeal or howl when a microphone is on

The room's speakers squeal, howl or ring when a microphone is on: the
microphone is picking up its own sound from the speakers (feedback). If the
microphone is not heard at all, follow
[Microphone cannot be heard in the room](rb-av-008-microphone-cannot-be-heard-in-the-room.md).

## Symptoms

- "The mic keeps screeching", "there's a loud whine when I walk around".
- A high-pitched tone that grows louder, often when the instructor walks
  under a speaker or toward the front.
- The squeal stops when the microphone is muted.

## Before you start

- **Time:** about 5 minutes.
- **Bring:** nothing.
- **Safety:** feedback can be loud enough to hurt: mute the microphone
  first (its own switch, or its mute on the touch panel), then find the
  cause. Leave its level where it is, so the checks can read it.
- **Do not:** change the audio processor's settings, or move speakers or
  the ceiling microphone.

## Checks

1. With the microphone muted, listen. **Expected:** the squeal has stopped.
   If it has not, the sound is not feedback from that microphone: go to
   [Escalate](#escalate).
2. Look at the microphone level on the touch panel. **Expected:** at or
   below the room's usual level (marked on the panel, or the middle of the
   scale). If it is higher, go to step 1 of [Fix](#fix).
3. Look at where the microphone is used. **Expected:** a lapel microphone
   on the chest, a handheld held near the mouth, and neither pointed at or
   held under a speaker. If not, go to step 2 of [Fix](#fix).
4. Count the microphones that are on. **Expected:** only the ones in use.
   If others are on, go to step 3 of [Fix](#fix).

## Fix

1. Set the microphone level back to the room's usual level, then unmute
   the microphone. **Expected:** the voice is heard without a squeal.
2. Clip the lapel microphone about a hand's width below the chin, or hold
   the handheld close to the mouth, and stay out from under the front
   speakers; then unmute it. **Expected:** no squeal at a normal speaking
   level.
3. Turn off or mute the microphones that are not in use, then unmute the
   one that is. **Expected:** no squeal.
4. If the room squeals at its usual level with one microphone used
   properly, the processor's settings are wrong: escalate, and leave the
   level low for the class.

## Escalate

Escalate if the checks do not find the fault or the fix does not hold: to
AV engineering, through the [escalation matrix](../../procedures/escalation-matrix.md).
Say which microphone, where in the room it squealed, and the panel level.

## Close the ticket

Record the room, what you found, what you did, and this runbook's
identifier, so a repeat fault can be traced.
