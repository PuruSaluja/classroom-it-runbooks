# Microphone cannot be heard in the room

The lectern's gooseneck microphone or a wireless handheld or lapel
microphone is not heard through the room's speakers. If no sound at all
comes from the speakers, video included, follow
[No sound from the room speakers](rb-av-007-no-sound-from-the-room-speakers.md);
if the microphone is heard but squeals, follow
[Speakers squeal or howl when a microphone is on](rb-av-010-speakers-squeal-or-howl-when-a-microphone-is-on.md).

## Symptoms

- "My mic isn't working", "the back row can't hear me".
- Videos play through the speakers but the instructor's voice does not.
- A wireless microphone's light is off, red or blinking, or its receiver
  in the rack shows no signal.

## Before you start

- **Time:** about 10 minutes.
- **Bring:** fresh AA batteries, or a charged spare microphone if the room
  uses rechargeable ones.
- **Safety:** turn the microphone volume down before you test, so a
  microphone that comes back does not squeal.
- **Do not:** change the receivers' channels or frequencies, or the audio
  processor's settings. A wrong channel can interfere with the next room.

## Checks

1. Look at the touch panel's microphone volume. **Expected:** not muted,
   and above the lowest level. If muted or at the bottom, go to step 1 of
   [Fix](#fix).
2. For a gooseneck microphone, look at the ring or light on its stem.
   **Expected:** lit, showing it is on. If it is off, go to step 2 of
   [Fix](#fix). For a wireless one, look at its power light. **Expected:**
   steady green, or its normal color. If it is off, red or blinking, go to
   step 3 of [Fix](#fix).
3. Look at the microphone's mute switch, if it has one. **Expected:** set
   to on, not mute. If it is set to mute, go to step 4 of [Fix](#fix).
4. Speak into the microphone from about a hand's width away. **Expected:**
   your voice from the speakers. If a wireless microphone is still not
   heard, go to step 5 of [Fix](#fix).

## Fix

1. Unmute the microphones on the panel and raise their level a few steps
   while someone speaks. **Expected:** the voice is heard clearly.
2. Press the gooseneck's button once. **Expected:** its light comes on and
   the voice is heard.
3. Put fresh batteries in the wireless microphone, or swap it for a charged
   spare from the lectern drawer, and turn it on. **Expected:** its power
   light is steady and the voice is heard.
4. Set the microphone's switch to on. **Expected:** the voice is heard.
5. Stand within sight of the lectern and speak again; if the room has two
   wireless microphones, try the other. **Expected:** the voice is heard.
   If one microphone works and the other does not, the fault is that
   microphone or its receiver: escalate.

## Escalate

Escalate if the checks do not find the fault or the fix does not hold: to
AV engineering, through the [escalation matrix](../../procedures/escalation-matrix.md).
Say which microphone, what its light showed, and whether fresh batteries
or the spare made a difference. Take a faulty wireless microphone out of
the room with a label saying so.

## Close the ticket

Record the room, what you found, what you did, and this runbook's
identifier, so a repeat fault can be traced.
